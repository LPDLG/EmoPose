# EmoPose

## Project Profile

This repository is used for presentations on the EmoPose framework for audio-driven portrait video generation. Here, we have organized the manuscript materials, the framework description, and the implementation records for generating continuous portrait videos from reference images and driving speech, with independently specified emotion and rigid head-pose conditions.

## EmoPose

With the continuous advances in generative artificial intelligence technologies, audio-driven portrait video generation has made significant progress in synthesizing natural facial motion from speech. However, existing audio-driven methods commonly derive facial expression and head motion implicitly from the audio signal, making it difficult to replace the emotion target or prescribe a time-varying head pose while keeping the audio unchanged. Repeated denoising in video diffusion models also increases the computational cost of interactive generation. In this paper, we propose EmoPose, an audio-driven portrait video generation framework for Chinese poetry recitation and general speech performance. The method combines a latent-consistency few-step generation process with low-rank adaptation, using a reference image and driving audio to provide identity and speech conditions. The emotion control module accepts three-class SER soft probabilities and maps them to context tokens shared across the latent video frames, providing a replaceable emotion target for the same audio. The rigid-pose control module accepts a six-component trajectory consisting of pitch, yaw, roll, face-center coordinates, and scale, aligns the trajectory from video frames to latent frames, and maps each latent frame to pose tokens that vary over time. Independent emotion and pose cross-attention branches are inserted at selected generation layers after audio attention and before the feed-forward transformation, and zero-initialized residual gates allow the two controls to enter the shared generation path without disrupting its initial behavior. The training procedure first learns the shared audio-conditioned few-step core and then fixes the learned core while training the emotion and rigid-pose control branches. Experiments on RAVDESS show that EmoPose preserves the identity of the reference subject and produces stable generation quality across random seeds, while the separate condition interfaces support emotion replacement and externally specified head motion for digital human recitation.
